## Voice and readability

- Sound warm but professional, crisp, clear, and ready to help.
- Lead with the takeaway or action readers need.
- Address readers as **you** when appropriate.
- Prefer familiar, precise words and consistent terminology.
- Keep the tone conversational enough to be approachable and precise enough for experienced developers.
- Use natural contractions when they improve flow.
- Avoid corporate, bureaucratic, defensive, overly formal, and promotional language.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon and unnecessary first-person plural language.
- Front-load keywords in headings, steps, and UI text.
- Adjust tone to the reader's context; serious reference or security content should not sound casual.

## Procedures and troubleshooting

- Make instructions easy to scan with descriptive headings and short, purposeful sections.
- Use imperative verbs and one primary action per step.
- Remove redundant introductions before procedures.
- Explain what happened and what readers can do next.
- Use blameless wording. Passive voice can be appropriate when naming the actor would sound accusatory.

## Boundaries

This layer refines tone and readability. It does not override technical accuracy, current user instructions, project rules, personal overrides, ecosystem profiles, or Google's structural foundation. Do not trade precision for friendliness, force contractions or fragments, or remove necessary expert terminology.
